Importing Furniture from China to Belgium: A First-Order Checklist

Belgium customs clearance is decided before the container reaches Antwerp-Bruges: who imports, which documents travel with the cargo, how duty and VAT land on your cost sheet, and which compliance duties sit on your side. Settle them before the deposit. If you do not, the container waits at the port and the fees keep running.

Who is the importer of record?

The importer of record is the legal entity that declares the goods into the EU and answers for the declaration. It needs two numbers:

  • EORI — the EU-wide customs identification number. First-time importers usually do not have one; apply through the Belgian customs authorities before the shipment is booked, not after it sails.
  • VAT number — determines where import VAT is declared and whether it can be deferred.

Three structures are common: your own Belgian entity (cleanest long-term); a licensed clearing agent as your customs representative (direct representation leaves you as declarant, while indirect representation puts the agent's name on the declaration and can make both parties liable for the customs debt); or a seller-side / logistics party importing (workable for a first order, but the goods enter the EU under someone else's identity).

Decide before signing the purchase contract: it dictates the invoice and the Incoterm.

The documents that decide clearance

Belgium customs clearance for furniture fails on documents more often than on inspections. Prepare this file before departure:

  • Commercial invoice — entities matching the importer of record, itemised descriptions (not "furniture"), unit prices, currency, Incoterm.
  • Packing list — piece counts, weights, carton dimensions, CBM.
  • Bill of lading / sea waybill — check consignee and notify party before sailing; amendments after arrival cost money.
  • HS code classification — agreed with supplier and broker in advance; furniture codes depend on material and function.
  • Certificate of origin.
  • ISPM 15 declaration — wooden crates and pallets, heat-treated and stamped; get written confirmation before packing.
  • EUDR due diligence file — for wooden furniture, see below.

A furniture sourcing agent can review the set before the container is sealed. Fixing an invoice in Shenzhen costs nothing; fixing it in Antwerp does not.

Duty, VAT, and the landed-cost trap

Duty is set by the HS code and the specific product. There is no single "furniture duty rate" — an upholstered armchair, an oak table, and a metal-frame bed classify differently. Confirm against the exact product; never budget off a number quoted for another item.

Import VAT in Belgium is the standard 21% on customs value plus duty. Whether it is paid at clearance or deferred depends on your registration and licences — ask your accountant; the cash-flow difference on a full container is real.

Wood packaging and EUDR

Two different obligations, often confused.

ISPM 15 is about the packaging: wooden pallets and crates must be heat-treated and stamped. It is the supplier's job at origin; unmarked wood packaging can be rejected.

EUDR is about the product: wooden furniture entering the EU needs due diligence showing the wood was legally harvested, with geolocation of the plot. The obligation sits on you, the importer — not the factory, not your forwarder. Your supplier provides the underlying information; you present the due diligence statement. EUDR is easier to handle at factory selection than at clearance. Our dedicated EUDR guide covers the practical steps.

Product safety and the EU responsible person

Do not assume furniture needs CE marking. CE applies to specific regulated categories; most standard household furniture — tables, chairs, sofas, beds — is not among them. A supplier claiming a sofa is "CE certified" is telling you nothing.

What does apply is EU general product safety law, including the GPSR framework: products must be safe, and a responsible economic operator established in the EU must be identifiable for them. As importer, part of that can fall on you — technical documentation, traceability, contact details on the product or packaging. Some categories — upholstered items, children's furniture — carry additional national or product-specific rules. Confirm obligations for your destination and product with a compliance adviser before ordering.

Antwerp-Bruges to your warehouse

  1. Arrival notice — issued to the notify party named on the bill of lading.
  2. D/O exchange — the bill of lading is exchanged for a delivery order against destination charges. If originals were not surrendered in time or a telex release arranged, this alone costs days.
  3. Clearance — your broker files the declaration. Timing depends on the route assigned: documentary, scan, or physical inspection. No honest forwarder promises fixed days.
  4. Release and haulage — the container goes to your warehouse, or to a CFS for destuffing first if LCL.
  5. Unloading — book dock, forklift, and labour in advance. If you cannot devan a 40ft container in one window, settle detention terms before pickup.

The most expensive mistake in an Antwerp furniture import is a container that is cleared, released — and has nowhere to go.

A first-order checklist

Before you pay the deposit:

  • ☐ Importer of record decided: your Belgian entity or a named representative
  • ☐ EORI obtained (or application in progress)
  • ☐ VAT and import-VAT handling confirmed with your accountant
  • ☐ HS codes agreed with supplier and broker for every line
  • ☐ Incoterm chosen deliberately — you know where your cost and risk start
  • ☐ Document set specified to the supplier, including ISPM 15 and EUDR for wooden items
  • ☐ EU responsible person / GPSR position clarified for your products
  • ☐ All-in landed cost estimated in writing
  • ☐ Pre-shipment QC arranged — independent inspection, photo/video evidence before balance payment
  • ☐ Warehouse slot, unloading labour, and container return plan ready

FAQ

Q: What is the minimum to prepare for a first furniture import into Belgium?

A: An importing entity with an EORI number, a confirmed VAT position, agreed HS codes, the full document set from your supplier, and a written all-in landed-cost estimate. Everything else can be refined on order two.

Q: Can my freight forwarder act as importer of record?

A: Some arrangements allow it, usually via indirect representation — but it shifts liability and documentation control to a third party, and not every forwarder offers it to first-time clients. Ask who signs the declaration and who bears liability before booking. For a serious programme, your own entity is the cleaner foundation.

Q: Does furniture need CE marking to enter Belgium?

A: Most standard household furniture does not fall under CE marking — it applies to specific regulated categories, not tables, chairs, and sofas in general. What always applies is the EU general product safety framework, including an identifiable responsible operator in the EU. Verify with a compliance adviser for your specific products.
