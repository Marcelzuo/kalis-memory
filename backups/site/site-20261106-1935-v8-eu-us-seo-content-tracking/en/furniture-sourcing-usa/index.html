What we do

The factory side, handled

Finding a factory is one step. The harder work is knowing which factory can hold the specification, catching a material substitution before the container is full, and getting the commercial documents to your broker before the filing clock runs out.

We work on beds, sofas, tables, sofa beds and chairs. We visit the factory, check the sample against the bulk order, and send you the evidence while there is still time to act on it.

We are a sourcing and inspection partner. We are not your importer of record, customs broker, testing laboratory or law firm. Those responsibilities stay with the professionals you appoint.

What we take off your desk

  • Factory matching. We compare the SKU, material, finish and order size against workshops that already build that category.
  • Sample and specification. We chase the approved sample, the bill of materials and the finish before mass production.
  • Production checks. Pre-production, mid-production and pre-shipment, with photos and video you can review.
  • Supplier data for entry. Commercial invoice detail, packing list, species and harvest country, and the factory declarations your broker asks for.
  • Freight coordination. Booking, loading supervision, seal check and the container number recorded against the file.
US entry

Six items to settle before the deposit

US clearance is category-specific. A sofa, a mattress and a child's chair do not carry the same compliance file. These are the items we ask about before the first order is placed.

ItemWhat it coversWho owns it
Importer of recordThe entity legally responsible for the entry, duties and compliance.You, or a qualified third party you appoint.
EIN and bondThe IRS number used as the importer number, plus a single-entry or continuous customs bond.You; your broker arranges the bond.
ISFThe 10+2 security filing, generally due at least 24 hours before loading.You as IOR; your broker usually files.
HTS and Section 301Classification by material and function, plus any additional duty on Chinese-origin goods.Your broker confirms the code and rate.
Lacey ActSpecies and harvest-country declaration for wood-containing products.You file; we collect the factory evidence.
CPSCCategory-specific safety rules, test reports and certificates where they apply.You, with your testing lab; we request supplier documents.

This table is a working checklist, not legal or customs advice. Rules and tariff lists change; confirm your position with your broker.

WEST

The main China-to-US furniture gateways. Plan chassis and drayage early; terminal and rail congestion can eat the free days.

Useful for East Coast distribution. Check the Panama routing and the inland haul before you compare rates.

An option for Texas and the central states. Confirm the forwarder's actual service and the warehouse appointment before booking.

Quality

Checks that happen before the port

Once the container is on the water, a missing part or a wrong finish becomes a US-side problem with US-side costs. We try to find it in the factory.

Pre-production sample

We review the first piece, the material and the finish before mass production. You approve what will be copied.

Mid-production

At roughly 30% completion we inspect again. Frame, foam, veneer, stitching and hardware are still fixable.

Pre-shipment

We check quantity, measurements, packing, labels and the loaded container. Photos and video go to you before balance.

Loading

We watch the loading, check the seal and record the container number. The document set should match what is inside.

Questions

US sourcing FAQ

Do I need a US entity and EIN to import furniture?
Usually yes if you are the importer of record. A foreign company can act as importer of record in some cases with a US agent, but it carries the same liability. Confirm the structure with your customs broker before you book.
Single-entry or continuous customs bond?
A single-entry bond covers one shipment. A continuous bond covers all entries for a year. If you expect several containers, ask your broker to price both.
Who files ISF?
The importer of record is responsible for the ISF filing, usually through its broker. We collect supplier and shipment data and send it to you or your broker; the filing must generally happen at least 24 hours before loading.
Can you tell me the duty rate for my furniture?
No. The rate depends on the exact HTS code, and goods of Chinese origin may also carry Section 301 duties. Your broker confirms the classification and the rate at entry. We help make sure the product data is complete enough to classify.
Do you handle the Lacey Act declaration?
The importer files the Lacey declaration. We ask the factory for the species and harvest country in writing and keep that evidence with the order file. We are not your customs broker or legal adviser.
Can you inspect goods before they leave China?
Yes. We check the pre-production sample, inspect at roughly 30% completion, and run a pre-shipment inspection with photos and video before the balance is released.
